# Session 5: Documents, research and verification

Level 2: Practitioner (25 to 50). Deliverable: Research brief with its verification table. Worth: 6 points.

Contents: Goal and outcomes, Key points, Plain-language glossary, A quote-first prompt, Four errors to catch, The verification table, Worked demo: a source-checked brief, Common mistakes, Cheat sheet, Exercises, Case to attempt, Vocabulary, Score ladder (what your work looks like at each level), Going further, Study sources, Self-check questions, Exit ticket.

## Goal and outcomes

**Goal.** The learner turns a pile of sources into a brief in which every claim has been checked.

**You will be able to:**
- Load long documents and ask in a way that surfaces evidence
- Separate what a source says, what Claude infers and what lies outside the sources
- Build a verification table: claim, source, checked by, status
- Catch premise errors and unsupported citations

## Key points

- Documents first, question last, and ask for quotes before conclusions.
- Knowledge stops at the cutoff. For current facts, use search or provided files and open the sources.
- Never accept a citation you have not opened. Check numbers against the source.
- Discernment is evaluating the output; Diligence is owning what you then send or publish.

## Plain-language glossary

| Term | Meaning | Think of it as |
|---|---|---|
| Source | A document or page you can open and check | The original, not a retelling |
| Claim | One statement that could be true or false | One row in the table |
| Evidence | The quote and the location that support a claim | The page number you can point to |
| Verification | Opening the source and comparing it with the claim | A second pair of eyes that is yours |
| Premise error | A false assumption hidden inside your question | A trick question |

## A quote-first prompt

1. Put the documents first.
2. Ask: for each claim, give the supporting quote and where it appears.
3. Ask Claude to mark anything it infers rather than reads.
4. Ask it to list what the documents do not cover.
5. Verify: open the source and compare.

Run this on one real document and keep the output for your verification table.

## Four errors to catch

| Error | What it looks like | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Fabricated citation | Real-looking author, journal and year | Search the title or DOI; unverified if not found |
| Outdated fact | True before the cutoff | Search on, then open an official dated page |
| Misread number | 1.2 million becomes 12 million | Compare with the table in the source |
| Unsupported inference | The claim goes beyond the source | Ask for the quote that supports it |

Link each error to a limit from session 1.

## The verification table

| Claim | Source | Checked by | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market grew 12% in 2025 | Industry report, p. 14 | Opened p. 14 | Confirmed |
| Competitor holds 40% share | None given | Not checked | Unsourced: find a source or remove |
| Smith (2024) shows the effect | DOI search | Searched title and DOI | Not found: unverified |

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Accepting a citation unopened | It may not exist or may not say that | Open every source that drives a decision |
| Current facts with search off | Training stops at the cutoff | Turn search on or supply files, then open the pages |
| Leading questions | Claude may continue a false premise | Ask neutrally and ask what would contradict the claim |
| Checking only the number | The surrounding sentence can change its meaning | Compare the whole sentence with the source |

## Cheat sheet

**Know**
- Documents first, question last, quotes before conclusions.
- Current facts need search or files.
- Open every citation. Check every number.
- Table: claim, source, checked by, status.

**Do**
- Ask for a brief with a quote and location for each claim.
- Open each source and compare.
- Fill the verification table.
- Remove or flag whatever is unsourced.

## Exercises

1. Source-checked brief. Take three documents. Ask for a brief where every claim carries a quote and its location. Build the verification table and log each error found.
2. Planted error. The instructor inserts a false statement into a document. The learner must catch it and note whether Claude flagged it.
3. Current-fact test. Ask five facts that changed this year with search off, then on, and verify against official pages.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quote first | Ask for the supporting quote and where it appears before any conclusion. |
| Cutoff | Knowledge stops there. For current facts use search or provided files, and open the sources. |
| Fabricated citation | A real-looking author, journal and year that does not exist. Search the title or DOI. |
| Misread number | 1.2 million becomes 12 million. Compare with the table in the source. |
| Discernment | Evaluating the output: is it right, and fit for use? |
| Diligence | Owning what you then send or publish. Anything unchecked is marked unverified. |

## Score ladder (what your work looks like at each level)

| Score | You can |
|---|---|
| 0 | Copies Claude's summary and sends it |
| 25 | Asks for quotes first and opens the sources |
| 50 | Builds a verification table and catches a planted error |
| 75 | Separates source, inference and outside claims and tests current facts with search off and on |
| 100 | Runs a repeatable research process with an audit trail that others can follow and challenge |

## Going further

- In every answer separate three things: what the source says, what Claude infers and what lies outside the sources.
- Plant an error in a test document to measure whether your process catches it. A process you never tested is a hope.
- Scale checking to the stakes: sample for background reading, verify everything for numbers in a decision.
- Keep the verification table with the brief so a reader can audit it later.

## Self-check questions

- Which claims in this brief would hurt most if wrong, and which source backs each?
- What would you do if the citation cannot be found?

## Exit ticket

Put this question to Claude, then paste its answer and your verdict on it:

> Which of the claims in your last answer are quoted from my sources, and which are your inference? (Use a real brief.)

**Now question it.** Take the claim you trust most and try to disprove it from the source. Did the source say it?
